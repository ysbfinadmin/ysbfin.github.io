M.S. & Ph.D. in Finance at Yonsei University (MS Finance Yonsei)

The Graduate Program in Finance at Yonsei School of Business offers rigorous, full-time Master of Science (M.S.), Integrated MS-Ph.D., and Ph.D. degrees in Finance. Our single-track academic curriculum trains students in theoretical and empirical Asset Pricing, Corporate Finance, Derivatives, and Insurance & Risk Management, preparing graduates for top global Ph.D. programs, academic faculty positions, and premier research institutions. Most courses are conducted in English.

02 / Specializations

Research Areas &
Prerequisites

Asset Pricing & Investments

Specialization 01

Theoretical and empirical research on the pricing of equities, fixed-income securities, and derivatives (futures & options), capital market efficiency, market microstructure, macro-finance, international finance, portfolio strategies, and performance evaluation.

Corporate Finance

Specialization 02

Theoretical and empirical analysis of corporate financial decisions aimed at firm value maximization, including capital raising, capital structure, payout policy, corporate investment, IPOs, mergers and acquisitions (M&A), corporate governance, and ESG.

Insurance & Risk Management

Specialization 03

Economic, managerial, statistical, and legal principles of insurance and risk management, and their implementation in insurance contract design, enterprise risk management, pension markets, longevity risk, financial regulation, and reinsurance.

Recommended Background

Preparation & Prerequisites
  • Financial Management, Financial Accounting, Microeconomics, Macroeconomics & Econometrics
  • Mathematics (Calculus, Linear Algebra) and Mathematical Statistics
  • Statistical & Econometric Programming (Python, R, SAS, STATA, etc.)
  • Proficiency in reading and writing academic research papers in English

04 / Academic Bylaws

Academic Regulations

Key coursework, qualifying examination, and graduation requirements.

Official Bylaws (PDF) ↓

1. Coursework & Credits (Ph.D.: 36 Credits / Integrated: 54 Credits; 9-Credit Minor Waived)

  • Methodology (12 Credits): Microeconomics for Finance, Mathematical Statistics, Econometrics I (9 required credits) + 1 methodology elective (3 credits).
  • Core Finance (15 Credits): Financial Analysis, Corporate Finance Theory I·II, Asset Pricing, and Empirical Finance (Qualifying Exam Subjects).
  • Major Electives (9 Credits): Graduate finance electives/seminars or approved courses in Economics, Applied Statistics, or Mathematics.

2. Qualifying Examination & Third Year Paper

  • Qualifying Exam: 180-minute written examination on the 5 Core Finance courses; 80% passing score (up to 2 attempts allowed).
  • Third Year Paper: Completion and successful defense of an independent Working Paper before a 3-faculty committee prior to dissertation proposal.

3. Dissertation Defense & Publication Requirements

  • Division Seminar: At least one presentation at the Finance Division Seminar prior to the final defense.
  • Research Output (Choose 1): ① International conference presentation (AFA, WFA, EFA, FMA, etc.), ② SSCI journal first-round review decision, or ③ Publication in a leading domestic finance journal.
  • Annual Report: Annual Research Progress Report submitted to the Division Chair starting from Year 2 (Ph.D.) or Year 3 (Integrated).

1. Coursework Requirements (4 Semesters, 30 Credits)

  • Core Finance (12 Credits): Financial Analysis, Corporate Finance Theory I, Asset Pricing, and Empirical Finance.
  • Methodology (9 Credits): Microeconomics for Finance, Mathematical Statistics, and Econometrics I.
  • Major Electives (9 Credits): Graduate finance electives or approved graduate courses in Economics/Statistics.

2. Methodology Exemption Policy

  • Exemption: Mathematical Statistics and/or Econometrics I may be waived if equivalent undergraduate courses were completed with a grade of B+ or higher (subject to Division Coordinator approval).

3. Seminar, Thesis & Transition Options

  • Division Seminar: Regular participation and completion of at least one research seminar course.
  • Master's Thesis: Written Master's thesis and successful oral defense under faculty supervision.
  • Integrated Track: Eligible to apply for transition to the Integrated MS-Ph.D. track before completing the 3rd semester.
